RescueFuel.

Turning a traumatic 10km walk into a blueprint for a legally compliant, high-revenue infrastructure project.

01. Overview

A Smart Shift: From Fuel Delivery to Emergency Rescue Service

RescueFuel is a local roadside assistance platform created to help people during fuel emergencies. The main goal is to support stranded vehicle users quickly and safely. Since direct petrol delivery has legal and safety restrictions in India, the idea was redesigned into a legally compliant emergency assistance service focused on helping users during roadside breakdown situations.

Target Audience

Daily commuters, highway travelers, and female solo drivers.

Key Skills
Product Strategy Crisis UX Real-time Systems Regulatory Navigation
02. The Challenge

The Empty Tank Problem.

Every year, many vehicle users in India get stranded because their fuel runs out unexpectedly.

The User Pain

Panic, physical exhaustion from pushing vehicles, and safety vulnerabilities—especially at night or on isolated highways.

The Legal Wall

Under PESO (Petroleum and Explosives Safety Organization) guidelines, selling and transporting loose petrol for commercial delivery is strictly prohibited.

The Goal

Create a solution that is 100% legal and 100% user-centric.

01. The Origin Story

The 10km Walk.

The Situation

It was around 7:15 PM on a small dark road near a village outside Namakkal. There were no street lights and not many people around. I was riding my scooty far from the main road when suddenly it started shaking and stopped. Then I noticed the fuel was almost empty.

The Problem

In a city, this might be a small problem. But in a village, it feels very difficult. I asked nearby houses for petrol, but nobody had any extra. The nearest petrol bunk was almost 10 km away. While pushing my scooty on the road, one thought came to my mind:

“If food can be delivered anywhere through apps, why can’t someone deliver just 2 liters of petrol?”

The Idea Moment

The problem was not just the long distance — there was also a legal issue. Petrol cannot be easily sold or carried in cans because of safety rules and fire risk. While walking and pushing my scooty, I started thinking seriously about this problem. That’s when I realized there is a big gap in rural areas, and if solved properly within the rules, it could become a useful service for many people.

The Solution

That 10 km walk became the starting point for RescueFuel. Instead of thinking only about selling petrol, I started thinking about creating a complete emergency support system for vehicle users. What felt like a difficult night slowly turned into an idea for a useful and legally safe service that could help many people in rural areas.

02. The Strategy

Engineering a Solution.

Strategy A

The Regulatory "Bypass"

The Barrier

PESO prohibits the commercial delivery of loose petrol in cans due to volatility.

The Solution

I architected a Roadside Assistance (RSA) Model where the fee is for the "Rescue Service," while fuel is provided as an emergency provision at pump cost.

Strategy B

Designing for "Panic Mode"

The Challenge

A stranded user has limited cognitive bandwidth and a dying phone battery.

The Logic

Stripped away all secondary features (coupons, ads). App opens directly to a Single-Tap SOS trigger with auto-GPS detection in < 5 seconds.

Strategy C

The "Assurance Loop"

The Logic

In a quiet village, waiting in the dark is the hardest part. Using a live telemetry feed, we show the "Rescue Hero" moving in real-time, providing Psychological Safety needed to prevent unsafe choices.

03. The Future

Scaling for Revenue.

The Grey Market Problem

Currently, rural users buy low-quality petrol in soda bottles from local shops at a 50% markup. This is dangerous and unregulated.

The Future Roadmap

As the government moves toward certifying "Mobile Fuel Dispensers," RescueFuel will already own the rider network, the user trust, and the data.

The Revenue Potential

Transitioning from a "Service Fee" model to a high-volume model creates a multi-crore revenue stream.

Revenue = (Service Fee + Margin) × Volume
